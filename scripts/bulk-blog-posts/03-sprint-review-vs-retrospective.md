# Sprint Review vs Retrospective: Key Differences Explained

Scrum teams run five events every sprint. Two of them — the sprint review and the sprint retrospective — happen at the end of the sprint and often get confused. Both involve looking back. Both happen in the final days of a sprint. Both can feel like "another meeting" when teams are tired.

But they serve fundamentally different purposes. Mixing them up leads to stakeholders sitting through process discussions they do not need to hear, or teams skipping honest reflection because the product demo ran long.

Understanding the difference between a sprint review and a sprint retrospective helps you run each event well, invite the right people, and get the outcomes each is designed to produce.

## Quick Comparison

| Aspect | Sprint Review | Sprint Retrospective |
|--------|---------------|---------------------|
| Primary focus | Product — what was built | Process — how the team worked |
| Audience | Stakeholders, customers, team | Development team only |
| Led by | Product Owner | Scrum Master (facilitator) |
| Key question | "Is this the right product?" | "How can we work better?" |
| Output | Backlog updates, feedback | Action items for improvement |
| Transparency | Outward — showing progress | Inward — team reflection |
| Typical duration | 1–4 hours (sprint-length dependent) | 1–1.5 hours |

The sprint review inspects the increment. The retrospective inspects the team. Both are essential. Neither replaces the other.

## What Is a Sprint Review?

The sprint review is a Scrum event where the team demonstrates completed work to stakeholders and gathers feedback. It is not a status report. It is a collaborative working session focused on the product.

### Purpose of the Sprint Review

The sprint review exists to:

- Inspect the increment delivered during the sprint
- Adapt the product backlog based on what was learned
- Align stakeholders and the team on what to build next
- Celebrate progress and maintain transparency

The Product Owner explains what was completed. The development team demonstrates working software (or whatever the increment is). Stakeholders ask questions, provide feedback, and discuss market changes or new priorities.

### Who Attends a Sprint Review?

- The Scrum Team (Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers)
- Stakeholders — customers, users, managers, partners, anyone with interest in the product
- Optionally: other teams affected by the work

The retrospective is team-only. The sprint review is deliberately inclusive. Stakeholders need visibility into progress, and the team needs direct feedback on whether they are building the right thing.

### What Happens in a Sprint Review

A typical sprint review agenda:

1. **Product Owner opens** — State the sprint goal and what was completed vs. planned
2. **Team demonstrates** — Show working increments, not slides about work
3. **Stakeholder feedback** — Questions, reactions, new ideas, concerns
4. **Backlog discussion** — Review upcoming priorities based on feedback
5. **Close** — Summarize decisions and next steps

The outcome is an updated understanding of the product direction, not a list of process improvements. Those belong in the retrospective.

### Sprint Review Best Practices

- Demo working software, not PowerPoint
- Focus on outcomes and user value, not task completion
- Welcome critical feedback without becoming defensive
- Timebox the event and stick to it
- Only show work that meets your Definition of Done

If your team struggles with what to demo, revisit your [sprint planning guide](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/sprint-planning-guide) to ensure sprint goals connect to demonstrable value.

## What Is a Sprint Retrospective?

The sprint retrospective is a Scrum event where the development team reflects on how they worked during the sprint and identifies improvements for the next one. It is the team's opportunity to inspect and adapt their process.

### Purpose of the Sprint Retrospective

The retrospective exists to:

- Reflect on what went well and what did not
- Identify root causes of problems, not just symptoms
- Agree on actionable improvements
- Strengthen team collaboration and trust

This is where teams discuss communication breakdowns, estimation misses, tooling frustrations, and collaboration wins. It is inward-facing and requires psychological safety to be effective.

### Who Attends a Sprint Retrospective?

Only the Scrum Team — Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers. Stakeholders do not attend. Managers generally do not attend unless they are active members of the development team.

Keeping the retrospective private encourages honesty. Team members are more likely to raise concerns about workload, interpersonal dynamics, or leadership decisions when the audience is limited to people who share responsibility for improvement.

### What Happens in a Sprint Retrospective

A typical retrospective follows a structured format. See our [60-minute retrospective agenda](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/facilitate-retrospective-60-minute-agenda) for a detailed walkthrough.

Common structure:

1. **Set the stage** — Remind the team of the retrospective prime directive: everyone did their best given what they knew
2. **Gather data** — Collect observations about the sprint using a format like [Start Stop Continue](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/start-stop-continue-retrospective-template) or [Mad Sad Glad](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/mad-sad-glad-retrospective)
3. **Generate insights** — Group themes, discuss patterns, identify root causes
4. **Decide what to do** — Choose one to three concrete action items
5. **Close** — Summarize commitments and appreciate contributions

The outcome is action items the team owns — not a backlog of product features. Effective retrospectives [turn feedback into action items](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/turn-retrospective-feedback-into-action-items) that carry into the next sprint.

### Retrospective Best Practices

- Use a consistent [frequency and length](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/retrospective-frequency-and-length) — typically end of every sprint, 60–90 minutes
- Rotate formats to keep engagement high
- Focus on a few actionable improvements, not a laundry list
- Follow up on previous action items before generating new ones
- Address [silence, conflict, and dominant voices](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/handle-silence-conflict-dominant-voices-retrospectives) proactively

Tools like SprintsPlans help teams run online retrospectives by gathering feedback in one place, enabling anonymous voting on priorities, and tracking action items across sprints.

## Why Teams Confuse the Two Events

Several factors blur the line between sprint reviews and retrospectives:

### Scheduling Back-to-Back

Teams often run both events on the same day. Fatigue from a long review leads to a rushed retrospective, or review conversations bleed into process topics.

**Fix:** Schedule a break between events. Keep agendas distinct. Assign a facilitator who redirects off-topic discussions.

### Stakeholder Overlap

When managers attend both events — or when the review becomes a forum for criticizing how the team works — the psychological safety of the retrospective erodes.

**Fix:** Be explicit about which event is for product feedback and which is for team reflection. Enforce the attendee list.

### Demo-Driven Reviews That Skip Feedback

If the sprint review is purely a demo with no stakeholder dialogue, teams may try to address product concerns in the retrospective instead.

**Fix:** Build feedback time into the review. Ask stakeholders direct questions about what they saw.

### Retrospectives That Discuss Product Direction

Teams sometimes use retrospective time to debate feature priorities — work that belongs in the review or backlog refinement.

**Fix:** Capture product ideas in a parking lot and route them to the Product Owner. Keep the retrospective focused on how the team works.

## How Sprint Review and Retrospective Work Together

These events are complementary parts of Scrum's inspect-and-adapt cycle.

**The sprint review** answers: Did we build the right thing? Stakeholder feedback from the review feeds the product backlog. The team learns whether their assumptions were correct.

**The retrospective** answers: Did we build it the right way? Team reflection feeds process improvements. The team learns whether their collaboration, tools, and practices supported delivery.

A team that excels at reviews but skips retrospectives ships features efficiently while repeating the same dysfunctions sprint after sprint. A team that retrospects well but demos poorly loses stakeholder trust and builds the wrong things confidently.

Both events need attention. Both produce different kinds of learning that make the next sprint better.

## Running Both Events in a Single Day

Many two-week sprint teams schedule the review and retrospective on the last day of the sprint. Here is a practical sequence:

| Time | Event | Duration |
|------|-------|----------|
| Morning | Sprint Review | 1–2 hours |
| Midday | Break | 30–60 minutes |
| Afternoon | Sprint Retrospective | 60–90 minutes |

The break matters. Stakeholder energy from the review is different from the reflective energy the retrospective requires. Give the team time to shift modes.

For remote teams, use separate video links or clearly labeled calendar invites. Mixing attendees between events is a common source of confusion.

## Sprint Review vs Retrospective for New Teams

If your team is running its [first sprint retrospective](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/sprint-retrospective-guide), establish the distinction early. New teams often lump all end-of-sprint reflection into one meeting because it feels efficient.

Resist that urge. Stakeholders who hear process complaints lose confidence. Team members who cannot speak freely in front of managers withhold the feedback that drives improvement.

Train stakeholders on what to expect from the review. Train the team on what the retrospective is for. Clarity at the start prevents months of muddled meetings.

## Common Mistakes

### Turning the Review into a Retrospective

Discussing estimation accuracy, communication gaps, or tooling problems in front of stakeholders shifts the review from product inspection to team therapy. Redirect process topics to the retrospective.

### Skipping the Retrospective After a Good Sprint

High-performing sprints still contain learning. Teams that only retrospect after failures miss opportunities to reinforce what works and incrementally improve.

### No Action Items from the Retrospective

A retrospective that generates insights without commitments is a venting session. Every retrospective should produce one to three owned action items with clear follow-up in the next sprint.

### Product Owner Dominating the Retrospective

The Product Owner attends the retrospective as a team member, not as the decision-maker. Facilitators should ensure all voices contribute equally.

### Review Without Stakeholders

A review with only the team present lacks the external perspective that makes the event valuable. If stakeholders consistently skip reviews, address attendance as a retrospective topic.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can we combine the sprint review and retrospective?

Scrum defines them as separate events for good reasons. Combining them compromises both outcomes. If time is limited, shorten each event rather than merging them.

### What if stakeholders give process feedback during the review?

Acknowledge it, capture it, and route it to the retrospective or a separate conversation. Do not solve team dynamics in front of an external audience.

### Does the Scrum Master present at the sprint review?

The development team demonstrates work. The Product Owner facilitates the review. The Scrum Master may attend but the focus is on the increment, not the process.

### How long should each event be?

For a two-week sprint, plan one hour for the review and one hour for the retrospective. Scale proportionally for longer sprints. See guidance on [retrospective frequency and length](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/retrospective-frequency-and-length).

### Is the retrospective confidential?

Yes, in practice. What is discussed should stay within the team unless the team explicitly agrees to share outcomes. This protects psychological safety.

### What retrospective format works best after a sprint review?

Formats that encourage reflection on collaboration work well — [Sailboat](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/sailboat-retrospective-guide), [4Ls](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/4ls-retrospective-template), or [Starfish](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/starfish-retrospective-template). Choose based on what the team needs to explore.

## Master Both Events for a Stronger Scrum Practice

The sprint review and sprint retrospective are not interchangeable end-of-sprint meetings. One looks outward at the product. One looks inward at the team. One invites stakeholders. One requires privacy.

Run them separately. Invite the right people. Produce the right outcomes. When your team demonstrates working increments to engaged stakeholders and then reflects honestly on how to improve, you get the full benefit of Scrum's inspect-and-adapt rhythm.
