# Rose Thorn Bud Retrospective: Template and Facilitation Guide

The best retrospective formats feel natural, not mechanical. Rose Thorn Bud uses a metaphor everyone understands: a rose has beautiful petals, sharp thorns, and promising buds waiting to open. Applied to team reflection, these three elements create a balanced conversation about the past, present, and future.

**Rose** represents what went well — successes, wins, and positive experiences.  
**Thorn** represents what was difficult — frustrations, obstacles, and pain points.  
**Bud** represents what is emerging — opportunities, ideas, and potential growth.

This format is visually intuitive, emotionally accessible, and particularly effective for teams that need a gentler entry into retrospective practice. It works in person, remotely, and across cultures where direct criticism feels uncomfortable.

## What Is the Rose Thorn Bud Retrospective?

Rose Thorn Bud is a three-column retrospective format that asks team members to reflect on:

| Element | Symbol | Question | Time Orientation |
|---------|--------|----------|------------------|
| Rose | 🌹 | What went well? | Past / present positive |
| Thorn | 🥀 | What was challenging or painful? | Past / present negative |
| Bud | 🌱 | What opportunities are emerging? | Future positive |

Unlike formats that focus only on problems or only on wins, Rose Thorn Bud creates space for all three dimensions in every retrospective. The Bud column is what sets it apart from simpler formats — it channels energy toward possibility rather than leaving the team in a thorn-heavy conversation.

## When to Use Rose Thorn Bud

This format shines in specific contexts:

- **Teams new to retrospectives** who respond well to metaphors over corporate language
- **Sprints with mixed outcomes** where both wins and struggles deserve attention
- **Teams building psychological safety** — the rose column establishes positivity before thorns
- **Creative or design teams** who appreciate visual, metaphor-driven exercises
- **End-of-quarter reflections** where buds represent opportunities for the next period
- **Remote teams** where a simple three-column board is easy to set up online

If your team needs deep root cause analysis on a specific failure, consider pairing Rose Thorn Bud with follow-up discussion using [root cause questions](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/sprint-retrospective-questions-root-causes). If emotions are running high, [Mad Sad Glad](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/mad-sad-glad-retrospective) may be a better fit.

## How to Facilitate Rose Thorn Bud

Allow 60–75 minutes for a standard sprint retrospective. Follow the structure outlined in our [60-minute retrospective agenda](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/facilitate-retrospective-60-minute-agenda).

### Preparation

Set up three columns on a whiteboard, wall, or digital board:

```
┌──────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┐
│     🌹       │     🥀       │     🌱       │
│    ROSE      │    THORN     │     BUD      │
│ What went    │ What was     │ What         │
│ well?        │ difficult?   │ opportunities│
│              │              │ are emerging?│
└──────────────┴──────────────┴──────────────┘
```

Prepare sticky notes in three colors if in person — red/pink for roses, green for thorns (or use a neutral color for all), and yellow/light green for buds. Color coding is optional but helps visual clustering.

### Step 1: Set the Stage (5 minutes)

Introduce the metaphor. Explain each column. Read the retrospective prime directive. Establish ground rules for respectful discussion.

A warm-up question helps: "Share one rose from this sprint — something that went well for you personally or the team."

### Step 2: Silent Reflection (10 minutes)

Ask team members to write at least one note for each column. Encourage multiple notes where inspiration strikes. Silent writing ensures [quieter voices](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/handle-silence-conflict-dominant-voices-retrospectives) contribute equally.

**Prompts:**

- **Rose:** What are you proud of? What exceeded expectations? What felt smooth?
- **Thorn:** What frustrated you? What took longer than expected? What created friction?
- **Bud:** What idea is starting to form? What could we try next sprint? What potential do you see?

### Step 3: Share and Cluster (15 minutes)

Go column by column, starting with **Rose**. Beginning with positives sets a constructive tone before thorns surface.

Have each person share their roses. Cluster similar themes. Acknowledge wins explicitly — teams that skip celebration lose morale over time.

Move to **Thorn**. Share and cluster. The facilitator should watch for thorns that are symptoms of deeper issues. Note these for the discussion phase.

Finish with **Bud**. Share and cluster. Buds often reveal team aspirations that become action items.

### Step 4: Discuss and Prioritize (25 minutes)

Focus discussion on the most significant clusters. Use dot voting to prioritize thorns and buds for deeper exploration.

**For thorns:** Ask why. Use follow-up questions from our [root cause guide](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/sprint-retrospective-questions-root-causes). Move from "deployments were stressful" to "we lack a rollback procedure."

**For buds:** Ask what it would take to nurture them. Move from "we should automate more" to "let us automate the staging deployment this sprint."

Roses inform what to protect. If a rose cluster reveals a practice worth preserving, note it as a Keep item — similar to the [KALM retrospective](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/kalm-retrospective-template) format.

### Step 5: Action Items and Close (10 minutes)

Generate one to three action items, primarily from thorns and buds. Roses rarely produce actions — they produce commitments to continue.

Assign owners. Document items where the team can track them. [SprintsPlans](https://sprintsplans.com) helps teams run Rose Thorn Bud retrospectives online, gather feedback from all members, vote anonymously on which thorns and buds to prioritize, and [turn insights into action items](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/turn-retrospective-feedback-into-action-items).

Close with a round of appreciation. Ask each person to share one rose they appreciated about a teammate.

## Rose Thorn Bud Examples

### Engineering Team After a Release Sprint

**Roses:**
- Shipped on schedule despite scope pressure
- QA and dev pair-tested critical flows together
- On-call rotation handled production issues smoothly

**Thorns:**
- Last-minute feature additions disrupted testing
- Documentation was written after deployment, not during
- Two team members worked overtime without the team knowing

**Buds:**
- Opportunity to establish a feature freeze policy
- Interest in shifting documentation into the Definition of Done
- Potential to improve visibility into individual workload

**Action items:**
1. Propose feature freeze 48 hours before release (Owner: Product Owner)
2. Add documentation to DoD checklist (Owner: Tech lead)
3. Share workload concerns in daily standup (Owner: Whole team)

### Remote Design Team

**Roses:**
- New design system components reduced inconsistency
- Client feedback on the prototype was overwhelmingly positive
- Async critique sessions worked better than expected

**Thorns:**
- Time zone gaps delayed feedback loops
- Asset handoff to development was unclear
- Two rounds of revisions were not budgeted in estimates

**Buds:**
- Opportunity to create a handoff checklist for dev
- Interest in overlapping hours for real-time collaboration
- Potential to involve developers earlier in the design process

## Rose Thorn Bud vs. Other Formats

| Format | Structure | Key Difference |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| Rose Thorn Bud | Positive, negative, emerging opportunity | Metaphor-driven, future-oriented Bud column |
| [Mad Sad Glad](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/mad-sad-glad-retrospective) | Emotional states | Focuses on feelings, not events |
| [4Ls](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/4ls-retrospective-template) | Liked, Learned, Lacked, Longed For | Learning emphasis |
| [Start Stop Continue](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/start-stop-continue-retrospective-template) | Action-oriented | Less emotional, more process-focused |
| [Sailboat](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/sailboat-retrospective-guide) | Wind, anchor, rocks, island | Goal-oriented metaphor |

Rose Thorn Bud is closest to Sailboat in using metaphor, but simpler. Three columns instead of four. Less setup, faster to explain.

## Facilitation Tips

### Do Not Rush the Roses

Teams eager to solve problems may skip the rose column. Insist on it. Research on team performance consistently shows that celebrating wins improves resilience and engagement. Roses are not filler — they are foundation.

### Thorns Are Not Personal Attacks

Reinforce that thorns describe experiences and systems, not people. "The handoff process was unclear" is a thorn. "Sarah did not communicate" is a personal attack. Redirect when needed.

### Nurture the Buds

The bud column prevents retrospectives from ending on a negative note. Even when thorns dominate, buds give the team something to look forward to. Facilitators should spend meaningful time here, not treat buds as an afterthought.

### Use Visual Elements

The rose metaphor works because it is visual. Add simple illustrations to your board. Use flower emoji in digital tools. The metaphor should be present throughout, not just in the column headers.

### Adapt for [Remote Teams](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/psychological-safety-remote-teams)

Remote facilitation requires intentional design. Use a digital board with three clear columns. Enable anonymous posting for thorns if safety is still developing. Allow extra time for sharing — video calls add latency.

## Common Mistakes

### All Thorns, No Roses or Buds

A thorn-heavy retrospective demoralizes the team. If this happens, extend the rose sharing phase or use a check-in question that forces positivity.

### Buds Without Action

Buds represent opportunity, but opportunity without action fades. Convert the top one or two buds into sprint commitments with owners.

### Skipping Clustering

Reading every note aloud without grouping themes wastes time. Cluster as you go. Name themes: "I see several notes about deployment — let us group those."

### Running Rose Thorn Bud Every Sprint

Rotation keeps retrospectives fresh. Alternate with [Starfish](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/starfish-retrospective-template), [Lean Coffee](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/lean-coffee-retrospective), or other formats every few sprints.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where did Rose Thorn Bud originate?

The format draws on a common reflection exercise used in education, coaching, and design thinking. It has been adapted widely in Agile communities for sprint retrospectives.

### Can we use more than three columns?

The power of Rose Thorn Bud is simplicity. If you need more dimensions, choose a different format like [Starfish](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/starfish-retrospective-template) or [4Ls](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/4ls-retrospective-template).

### How do we handle sensitive thorns?

Create safety through the prime directive, anonymous input, and facilitator intervention. For deeply sensitive topics, schedule a follow-up conversation with relevant parties rather than forcing resolution in the retro.

### Is this format suitable for large teams?

It works for teams up to about 12 people. Larger teams should split into sub-groups, run Rose Thorn Bud independently, then merge themes.

### How does Rose Thorn Bud support continuous improvement?

Regular use builds a habit of balanced reflection — celebrating wins, addressing pain, and investing in growth. Over time, this feeds a [continuous improvement system](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/continuous-improvement-system-for-teams) where the team compounds learning sprint over sprint.

## Try Rose Thorn Bud in Your Next Retrospective

Rose Thorn Bud offers teams a natural, balanced way to reflect. Roses ground the conversation in what works. Thorns surface what needs attention. Buds point toward what is possible.

The metaphor is simple enough to explain in two minutes and rich enough to sustain a full retrospective. Whether your team is running its [first sprint retrospective](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/sprint-retrospective-guide) or looking for a fresh format to re-engage a tired group, Rose Thorn Bud delivers structured reflection without corporate stiffness.

Set up three columns. Gather roses, thorns, and buds. Discuss, prioritize, and commit. Then follow through — because the bud only blooms when the team nurtures it.
