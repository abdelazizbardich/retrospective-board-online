# Three Little Pigs Retrospective Game for Stronger Teams

Every team builds houses. Some practices are straw — they look fine until pressure arrives, then they collapse. Some are sticks — adequate for normal conditions but shaky under stress. And some are bricks — solid foundations that hold up sprint after sprint.

The Three Little Pigs retrospective game uses this childhood metaphor to help teams honestly assess the strength of their practices, tools, processes, and collaboration patterns. It is engaging, visually intuitive, and particularly effective for teams that need to discuss resilience and sustainability without the dryness of a process audit.

## What Is the Three Little Pigs Retrospective?

The Three Little Pigs retrospective is an Agile team activity based on the classic fairy tale. Team members categorize aspects of their work into three houses:

| House | Material | Meaning | Question |
|-------|----------|---------|----------|
| 🏚️ **House of Straw** | Fragile | Things that will not survive pressure | What is broken or barely holding together? |
| 🏠 **House of Sticks** | Adequate | Things that work but are vulnerable | What is okay but needs strengthening? |
| 🏰 **House of Bricks** | Strong | Things that are solid and reliable | What is robust and worth protecting? |

The metaphor makes vulnerability discussable. Saying "our deployment process is a house of straw" is easier than saying "our deployment process is terrible" — the fairy tale frame creates distance that enables honesty.

## When to Use the Three Little Pigs Game

This retrospective game works especially well when:

- **The team faced a stressful sprint** — a failed release, production incident, or crunch period that exposed weaknesses
- **You want to assess resilience** — not just what went wrong, but what would survive the next storm
- **Onboarding discussions** — new team members quickly understand which practices are solid and which are fragile
- **Post-incident retrospectives** — the metaphor naturally maps to "what failed under pressure?"
- **Teams that enjoy playful formats** — the fairy tale theme engages people who find standard retrospectives boring
- **Quarterly health reviews** — assess the structural strength of team practices over time

For emotionally charged sprints, consider pairing with or starting with [Mad Sad Glad](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/mad-sad-glad-retrospective). For root cause analysis on a specific incident, follow the Three Little Pigs discussion with [root cause questions](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/sprint-retrospective-questions-root-causes).

## How to Facilitate the Three Little Pigs Retrospective

Plan 60–75 minutes. Use the five-phase structure from our [60-minute retrospective agenda](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/facilitate-retrospective-60-minute-agenda).

### Step 1: Set the Stage (5 minutes)

Tell the story briefly — three pigs, three houses, the wolf. Everyone knows it. Connect the metaphor:

- **Straw houses** collapse at the first challenge
- **Stick houses** hold up to moderate pressure but fail under serious stress
- **Brick houses** survive whatever the wolf brings

Explain that the wolf represents sprint pressure — deadlines, bugs, scope changes, team absences, production incidents.

Establish ground rules. Read the retrospective prime directive.

### Step 2: Build the Three Houses (10 minutes)

Display three sections on your board:

```
┌─────────────────┬─────────────────┬─────────────────┐
│  🏚️ HOUSE OF    │  🏠 HOUSE OF     │  🏰 HOUSE OF    │
│     STRAW       │     STICKS      │     BRICKS      │
│                 │                 │                 │
│  Fragile —      │  Adequate —     │  Strong —       │
│  won't survive  │  works but      │  solid and      │
│  pressure       │  vulnerable     │  reliable       │
└─────────────────┴─────────────────┴─────────────────┘
```

Ask team members to write practices, tools, processes, or team dynamics on sticky notes and place them in the appropriate house. Silent writing for 8–10 minutes.

**Prompts:**

- **Straw:** What would break if we had a bad week? What do we do that we know is not sustainable?
- **Sticks:** What works on normal days but worried you during the last stressful sprint?
- **Bricks:** What held up under pressure? What can we count on?

Encourage honesty. Straw houses are the most valuable column — they reveal what needs urgent attention.

### Step 3: Share and Discuss (25 minutes)

Walk through each house, starting with **Bricks** to establish what is working.

**House of Bricks discussion:**
- What makes these practices strong?
- How do we protect them from erosion?
- Are any brick houses actually sticks we have been overestimating?

**House of Sticks discussion:**
- What would it take to upgrade these to bricks?
- Which sticks are closest to becoming straw?
- Are we investing enough in strengthening these?

**House of Straw discussion:**
- This is where the real work happens. Why are these still straw?
- What would a brick version look like?
- Which straw houses are most dangerous right now?

The facilitator probes without blaming. "Our testing process is straw" is about the process, not the tester. Maintain [accountability without blame](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/accountability-without-blame).

Watch for disagreements — one person's brick may be another's stick. These perception gaps are valuable discussion topics.

### Step 4: Prioritize and Plan (15 minutes)

You cannot rebuild every house in one sprint. Prioritize:

1. **Urgent straw** — What will cause the most damage if the wolf comes next sprint?
2. **High-value stick upgrades** — What strengthening effort gives the best return?
3. **Brick protection** — What must we not let erode?

Use dot voting or anonymous voting to select priorities. Tools like [SprintsPlans](https://sprintsplans.com) let teams organize Three Little Pigs notes by house, vote anonymously on the most critical straw houses, and assign action items.

Generate one to three action items:

| House | Action Type | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| Straw | Rebuild | "Create automated rollback procedure by end of sprint" |
| Sticks | Strengthen | "Add integration tests for the three most-changed modules" |
| Bricks | Protect | "Document our code review process in team handbook" |

[Turn retrospective feedback into action items](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/turn-retrospective-feedback-into-action-items) with clear owners and success criteria.

### Step 5: Close (5 minutes)

Summarize the three houses. Acknowledge the team's honesty about straw houses — that takes courage. End with appreciation for brick houses that held the team together.

Optional closing: "If the wolf came tomorrow, which straw house would we lose first?" This sharpens urgency without creating panic.

## Three Little Pigs Examples

### Software Team After a Production Incident

**House of Straw:**
- Manual deployment checklist (one missed step caused the incident)
- On-call runbook (outdated, team relied on tribal knowledge)
- Cross-team communication during incidents

**House of Sticks:**
- Automated test suite (good coverage but slow, team skips tests under pressure)
- Sprint planning (works normally but breaks down when scope changes mid-sprint)
- Monitoring alerts (exist but too noisy to trust)

**House of Bricks:**
- Code review culture (caught two bugs before merge this sprint)
- Blameless post-incident process
- Team communication in Slack during the incident

**Action items:**
1. Automate deployment checklist (straw → brick) — Owner: DevOps engineer
2. Add fast smoke test suite for pre-deploy validation (sticks → brick) — Owner: QA lead
3. Triage and reduce alert noise by 50% (sticks → brick) — Owner: On-call lead

### Marketing Team During Peak Season

**House of Straw:**
- Content approval workflow (bottlenecked under volume)
- Asset version control (teams working from outdated files)
- Capacity planning for seasonal peaks

**House of Sticks:**
- Campaign performance reporting (manual, time-consuming, but accurate)
- Cross-channel messaging consistency
- Freelancer onboarding process

**House of Bricks:**
- Core brand guidelines (everyone references them reliably)
- Weekly team sync rhythm
- Relationship with key agency partners

## Three Little Pigs vs. Other Retrospective Games

| Activity | Metaphor | Focus | Best For |
|----------|----------|-------|----------|
| Three Little Pigs | House materials | Resilience and stability | Stress-testing practices |
| [Sailboat](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/sailboat-retrospective-guide) | Wind, anchor, rocks, island | Progress toward goals | Goal-oriented teams |
| [Timeline](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/timeline-retrospective-activity) | Chronological events | Sequence and causality | Turbulent sprints |
| [Rose Thorn Bud](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/rose-thorn-bud-retrospective-template) | Flower metaphor | Balanced positive/negative | General reflection |
| [ESVP](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/esvp-retrospective-exercise) | Participation roles | Engagement check | Pre-retro warmup |

Three Little Pigs is unique in assessing structural strength. Other formats ask what happened or how people feel. This game asks what will survive the next challenge.

## Facilitation Tips

### Celebrate Brick Houses

Teams that only discuss straw and sticks lose sight of what works. Spend genuine time on bricks. Reinforce practices worth protecting.

### Straw Is Not Shame

Frame straw houses as opportunities, not failures. Every team has straw houses. The game's value is making them visible before the wolf arrives.

### Beware False Bricks

Some practices feel solid until tested. Ask: "Was this truly a brick this sprint, or did we get lucky?" The [timeline retrospective](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/timeline-retrospective-activity) can validate whether brick houses actually held under pressure.

### Use Visual Elements

Draw simple houses on the board. Use the fairy tale theme lightly — enough to engage, not so much that it feels childish. A small wolf sketch near the straw house column adds humor without undermining seriousness.

### Handle [Dominant Voices](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/handle-silence-conflict-dominant-voices-retrospectives)

Silent writing before sharing prevents the team's loudest member from pre-categorizing everything. Anonymous placement of notes works for sensitive topics.

### Remote Facilitation

Use a digital board with three clearly labeled sections. The visual metaphor translates well online. Anonymous input helps remote teams where [psychological safety](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/psychological-safety-remote-teams) is harder to establish.

## Running Three Little Pigs as a Recurring Activity

Track your houses over time for powerful trend data:

| Sprint | Straw | Sticks | Bricks | Trend |
|--------|-------|--------|--------|-------|
| 10 | 5 | 4 | 3 | Baseline |
| 11 | 4 | 4 | 4 | Improving |
| 12 | 3 | 3 | 5 | Strong progress |
| 13 | 2 | 4 | 5 | Sticks need attention |

A shrinking straw column and growing brick column shows genuine resilience improvement. Persistent straw items signal action items that are not being followed through.

Integrate with your [continuous improvement system](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/continuous-improvement-system-for-teams) and periodic [team health checks](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/team-health-checks).

## Common Mistakes

### All Straw, No Bricks

A retrospective that only surfaces problems demoralizes the team. If nobody writes brick notes, prompt: "What held up during our hardest moment this sprint?"

### No Action on Straw

Identifying straw houses without rebuilding plans is just venting. Every Three Little Pigs retro must produce at least one action item targeting a straw or stick house.

### Taking the Metaphor Too Far

The fairy tale is a framing device, not a performance. A few visual elements help. A full costume reenactment does not.

### Running It Every Sprint

Rotate with other formats. Three Little Pigs every sprint loses impact. Use it when resilience is the theme — after incidents, during planning for high-pressure periods, or quarterly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the Three Little Pigs retrospective too playful for serious teams?

The metaphor creates psychological distance that helps serious teams discuss vulnerabilities they would otherwise avoid. Playfulness serves honesty, not the other way around.

### Can we add a fourth category?

The power is in three clear levels. If you need more nuance, use [Starfish](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/starfish-retrospective-template) or [DAKI](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/daki-retrospective-template) instead.

### How does this differ from a risk assessment?

Risk assessments identify potential problems. Three Little Pigs assesses current structural strength — what is already fragile, adequate, or strong in your actual practices.

### What if the team disagrees on house placement?

Discussion is the point. Debate whether testing is straw or sticks reveals different perspectives and assumptions. Facilitate the conversation, then vote if needed.

### Can new teams use this format?

Yes. It is intuitive and does not require retrospective experience. For first-time teams, pair with our [first sprint retrospective guide](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/first-sprint-retrospective-guide).

### How do we prevent straw houses from recurring?

Track them across retrospectives. If the same practice stays straw sprint after sprint, escalate from "improve" to "drop and replace" — a conversation suited to the [DAKI retrospective](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/daki-retrospective-template).

## Build Brick Houses Before the Wolf Arrives

The Three Little Pigs retrospective game gives teams a memorable, honest framework for assessing resilience. Straw houses are not failures — they are warnings. Stick houses are not disasters — they are opportunities. Brick houses are not accidents — they are practices worth protecting and replicating.

Every sprint brings a wolf. Deadlines howl. Bugs blow. Scope changes huff and puff. The teams that survive are not the ones with the most straw — they are the ones who know which houses need rebuilding before the storm hits.

Set up three houses. Sort your practices. Discuss honestly. Rebuild what is fragile. Strengthen what is adequate. Protect what is solid.

Then face the next sprint knowing exactly what will stand and what will not.
