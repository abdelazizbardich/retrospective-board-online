# SprintsPlans vs EasyRetro: Which Retrospective Tool Fits Your Team?

Choosing a retrospective tool sounds simple until your team has tried three apps and still ends up in a shared document with no follow-through. EasyRetro and SprintsPlans both target Agile teams running online sprint retrospectives — but they emphasize different parts of the retro workflow.

This comparison helps Scrum Masters, team leads, and product owners evaluate **SprintsPlans vs EasyRetro** based on how teams actually run retros: gathering honest feedback, prioritizing themes, facilitating discussion, and turning insights into completed improvements.

We focus on practical fit — team size, remote habits, facilitation style, and whether you need structure or flexibility — not feature checklist marketing.

## What Teams Need from a Retrospective Tool

Before comparing products, clarify what your team needs. Most effective retrospective tools support:

- **Structured input** — Cards, columns, or templates so thinking is organized
- **Anonymous contribution and voting** — Safer feedback when hierarchy or personality skews discussion
- **Facilitation flow** — Timers, phases, and visibility for gather → group → vote → act
- **Remote participation** — Works for distributed and hybrid teams without friction
- **Action tracking** — Owners and deadlines that survive after the meeting ends
- **Format variety** — Different templates when retros feel repetitive

Teams that only need a whiteboard for sticky notes may prefer general tools. Teams that retro every sprint usually want purpose-built software. Our guide on [how to choose an online retrospective tool](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/choose-online-retrospective-tool) walks through that decision in more depth.

## SprintsPlans Overview

[SprintsPlans](https://sprintsplans.com/) is an online retrospective tool built for Agile teams that want a focused flow from feedback to action. Teams use it to run sprint retrospectives, gather input from everyone in the room or remotely, vote anonymously on what matters most, and capture action items tied to owners.

SprintsPlans fits teams that:

- Run regular Scrum or Kanban retrospectives
- Need anonymous voting without awkward reveal moments
- Want lightweight setup without configuring a general-purpose board
- Care about closing the loop on improvement items across sprints
- Prefer retrospective-specific workflows over adapting a whiteboard

It sits alongside other purpose-built retro tools in the landscape covered by [anonymous retrospective tools for Agile teams](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/10-best-anonymous-retrospective-tools-for-agile-teams-in-2026) — emphasizing clarity and follow-through rather than infinite customization.

## EasyRetro Overview

EasyRetro is a well-known retrospective tool offering board-based retro formats, team management, and integrations common in the Agile tooling space. Many teams adopt it for its familiar column layout, template library, and straightforward onboarding for facilitators who have used similar retro apps.

EasyRetro tends to appeal to teams that:

- Want a established retro brand with recognizable templates
- Already use workflows shaped around classic column boards (Glad/Sad/Mad, Start/Stop/Continue, etc.)
- Need team and board organization features for multiple squads
- Compare options actively against other retro-first tools in the market

EasyRetro is often evaluated alongside Parabol, TeamRetro, and newer entrants when teams refresh their tooling stack.

## Anonymous Feedback and Psychological Safety

Anonymous retrospectives reduce the risk that junior engineers, remote participants, or quieter teammates withhold concerns. Both tools support anonymous contribution patterns appropriate for retro culture.

The difference is often **workflow emphasis**. SprintsPlans centers anonymous gathering and voting in the default path — useful when teams struggle with [dominant voices](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/handle-silence-conflict-dominant-voices-retrospectives) or need safer [psychological safety on remote teams](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/psychological-safety-remote-teams).

EasyRetro's board model may feel more natural if your team already visualizes retro as columns and drag-and-drop cards. Facilitators should still configure anonymity and reveal timing deliberately — anonymity is a practice, not only a setting.

## Templates and Retrospective Formats

Format fatigue is real. Teams that run the same three-column retro monthly see participation drop.

EasyRetro's strength is template breadth for classic formats — teams can rotate Start/Stop/Continue, sailboat, or custom boards without rebuilding structure each time. That aligns with guides like the [Start/Stop/Continue template](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/start-stop-continue-retrospective-template) and [sailboat retrospective](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/sailboat-retrospective-guide).

SprintsPlans focuses on retrospective workflows that support rotation and prioritization without requiring facilitators to design board layout from scratch every session. Teams using [retrospective fatigue solutions](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/retrospective-fatigue-solutions) may prefer whichever tool makes experimenting with formats fastest for their facilitator style.

Neither tool replaces facilitation skill. The best template fails without a clear agenda — see [facilitating a 60-minute retrospective](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/facilitate-retrospective-60-minute-agenda) for timing that works regardless of software.

## Action Items and Improvement Follow-Through

The retrospective ends when actions complete — not when the meeting adjourns.

SprintsPlans emphasizes capturing action items with ownership as part of the retro flow, connecting naturally to practices like a [team improvement backlog](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/team-improvement-backlog) and [measuring retrospective effectiveness](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/measure-retrospective-effectiveness). Teams that lose actions in chat threads benefit from tooling that keeps improvements visible.

EasyRetro supports action items within its retro sessions. Teams should verify how actions export to Jira, Linear, or their task system — and whether open actions remain visible across sprints without manual copy-paste.

If your core pain is "we never do what we say," prioritize trials that test **action tracking across two sprints**, not just the in-meeting experience.

## Remote, Hybrid, and Async Considerations

Distributed teams should trial tools under real conditions: multiple time zones, partial attendance, and occasional [async retrospectives](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/async-retrospectives-remote-teams).

| Scenario | Consideration |
|----------|---------------|
| Fully remote | Mobile-friendly input, clear voting, stable video-side UX |
| Hybrid room | Easy join link, equal visibility for in-room and remote voices |
| Async-first | Input windows before live discussion; deadline clarity |
| Multi-timezone | [Time zone challenges](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/time-zone-retrospective-challenges) may favor async gather phases |

Both SprintsPlans and EasyRetro serve remote teams. Your team's sync vs async culture should drive weighting more than generic "remote ready" labels.

## Pricing and Team Size

Retrospective tools typically price by facilitator count, team size, or organization tier. Small teams often start with free or low-cost plans; growing organizations pay for admin, SSO, or multiple boards.

Rather than relying on comparison articles for numbers, run parallel trials during two real retrospectives and note:

- Does the free tier cap participants or history?
- Do you need multiple team workspaces?
- Will security review require SSO or data residency documentation?

For budget-conscious squads, also review [best free retrospective tools for small teams](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/best-free-retrospective-tools-2026) — SprintsPlans and EasyRetro may both appear depending on current offerings.

## Decision Framework: Choose SprintsPlans If…

- Anonymous voting and prioritization are central to your retro culture
- You want a streamlined path from feedback to assigned actions
- Facilitators prefer retrospective-specific flows over board configuration
- Follow-through and improvement tracking matter as much as the meeting itself
- You are establishing retro practice on a small or mid-size Agile team

## Decision Framework: Choose EasyRetro If…

- Your facilitators strongly prefer classic retro board templates out of the box
- You want a widely recognized retro tool with a large template habit in the community
- Board-based drag-and-drop matches how your team already thinks about retros
- You are comparing established retro vendors and EasyRetro fits your admin needs

## Decision Framework: Consider Neither (Yet) If…

- The team has not agreed on retro purpose or cadence — fix [what a retrospective is](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/what-is-a-retrospective) first
- Participation problems are cultural, not tooling — invest in facilitation and safety
- You retro quarterly without sprint rhythm — a document may suffice until cadence increases

## Trial Checklist for Both Tools

Run this checklist across a two-sprint trial with SprintsPlans and EasyRetro:

1. **Setup time** — How long until the team can join and contribute?
2. **Contribution breadth** — Did quieter members add input?
3. **Voting clarity** — Could the team prioritize without confusion?
4. **Discussion quality** — Did the tool help or distract during conversation?
5. **Action capture** — Owners and deadlines recorded where the team looks daily
6. **Second-sprint visibility** — Were last sprint's actions visible at retro open?
7. **Facilitator energy** — Did the Scrum Master spend time on tooling or facilitation?

Score honestly. The winning tool is the one that reduces friction on items three, five, and six — not the one with the longest feature page.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is SprintsPlans or EasyRetro better for small teams?

Both work for small teams. SprintsPlans may fit squads prioritizing simple anonymous flow and action follow-through; EasyRetro may fit teams wanting familiar board templates. Trial both on [free tiers](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/best-free-retrospective-tools-2026) if available.

### Can we switch tools later?

Yes. Retro habits transfer faster than feature knowledge. Document your formats and action tracking conventions so migration is painless.

### Do we need a dedicated tool if we have Miro?

Many teams start on Miro. Purpose-built tools reduce setup and improve action tracking. See [SprintsPlans vs Miro](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/sprintsplans-vs-miro-retrospectives) for when upgrade makes sense.

### Which tool is better for Scrum Masters?

The better tool supports the Scrum Master's facilitation goals: equal voice, clear priorities, actionable outcomes. Choose based on trial retro quality, not brand alone.

### Should action items live in the retro tool or Jira?

Often both: capture in retro for clarity, sync to Jira for execution. Test export or manual workflow during trial.

### How important is anonymous voting?

High for many teams — especially with hierarchy, remote imbalance, or recurring [silence problems](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/handle-silence-conflict-dominant-voices-retrospectives). Less critical for very small teams with strong safety already.

## Pick the Tool That Closes the Loop

SprintsPlans and EasyRetro both help Agile teams run online retrospectives better than a blank document. SprintsPlans shines when anonymous prioritization and action follow-through are the bottleneck. EasyRetro shines when facilitators want established board templates and a familiar retro-app experience.

Run a real trial, measure action completion, and ask whether the tool made quieter voices easier to hear. The right choice is the one your team uses every sprint — and the one that leaves improvement items closed, not orphaned.

Start your evaluation with one upcoming retrospective. Test gather, vote, discuss, and assign actions in each tool. Your next sprint's improvement backlog will tell you which platform earned its place.
