# KALM Retrospective Template: Keep, Add, Less, More

Most retrospective formats ask teams what went well and what did not. The KALM retrospective goes further. Instead of binary good-or-bad thinking, it invites teams to consider four distinct dimensions of change: what to preserve, what to introduce, what to reduce, and what to amplify.

KALM stands for **Keep, Add, Less, More**. The format is intuitive enough for new teams yet structured enough to produce actionable insights. It works especially well when a team feels stuck in repetitive retrospective conversations or when you want to move beyond simple Start-Stop-Continue thinking.

This guide explains how KALM works, when to use it, and how to facilitate it effectively.

## What Is the KALM Retrospective?

The KALM retrospective is an Agile reflection format with four columns:

| Column | Question | Focus |
|--------|----------|-------|
| **Keep** | What worked well that we should continue? | Preserve strengths |
| **Add** | What is missing that we should introduce? | New practices or tools |
| **Less** | What should we reduce or do less of? | Minimize waste or friction |
| **More** | What should we amplify or do more of? | Scale what is working |

Each column prompts a different type of thinking. "Keep" celebrates success. "Add" opens possibility. "Less" targets waste without demanding elimination. "More" builds on momentum.

The format shares DNA with [Start Stop Continue](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/start-stop-continue-retrospective-template) but adds nuance. "Less" is gentler than "Stop" — useful when the team needs to reduce something without abandoning it entirely. "More" explicitly asks teams to invest in what is already working, not just fix problems.

## When to Use the KALM Format

KALM works well in several situations:

- **Teams new to retrospectives** who need clear structure without complexity
- **Sprints that went reasonably well** where pure problem-finding feels forced
- **Process improvement focus** when you want balanced input across all four dimensions
- **Teams stuck in complaint loops** where retrospectives become negative by default
- **Mid-sprint check-ins** adapted as a lighter reflection exercise

Consider a different format if your team needs deep emotional processing ([Mad Sad Glad](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/mad-sad-glad-retrospective)) or root cause analysis on a specific incident. KALM is a general-purpose format suited to regular sprint retrospectives.

## How to Facilitate a KALM Retrospective

Plan 60–75 minutes for a standard two-week sprint retrospective. Follow the five-phase structure recommended in our [60-minute retrospective agenda](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/facilitate-retrospective-60-minute-agenda).

### Step 1: Set the Stage (5 minutes)

Welcome the team. Explain the four columns briefly. Remind everyone of the retrospective prime directive — everyone did their best given what they knew.

Display the four columns visibly:

```
┌─────────┬─────────┬─────────┬─────────┐
│  KEEP   │   ADD   │  LESS   │  MORE   │
│         │         │         │         │
└─────────┴─────────┴─────────┴─────────┘
```

### Step 2: Individual Brainstorming (10 minutes)

Ask team members to write one idea per sticky note (or digital card) for each column. They do not need to fill every column — but encourage at least one note per column if possible.

**Prompts to help:**

- **Keep:** What practice, habit, or decision helped us this sprint?
- **Add:** What tool, ritual, or support would have made a difference?
- **Less:** What meeting, process step, or behavior created unnecessary friction?
- **More:** What successful behavior should we double down on?

Silent writing prevents [dominant voices](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/handle-silence-conflict-dominant-voices-retrospectives) from shaping the conversation before everyone contributes.

### Step 3: Share and Cluster (15 minutes)

Have each person share their notes, column by column. Place them on the board. Group similar items together. The facilitator names themes as they emerge.

Watch for contradictions — one person may put "daily standups" in Keep while another puts them in Less. These tensions are valuable discussion starters, not problems to resolve immediately.

### Step 4: Discuss and Prioritize (25 minutes)

Focus discussion on the highest-impact clusters. Use dot voting or anonymous voting to identify priorities. Two to three votes per person usually surfaces the most important themes.

Ask probing questions:

- For **Keep** items: How do we make sure these do not erode over time?
- For **Add** items: What would it take to implement this next sprint?
- For **Less** items: Can we reduce this without losing something important?
- For **More** items: What specifically would "more" look like in practice?

Dig into root causes when patterns appear. Our guide to [sprint retrospective questions for root causes](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/sprint-retrospective-questions-root-causes) offers useful follow-up prompts.

### Step 5: Action Items and Close (10 minutes)

Translate the top priorities into one to three action items. Each item needs an owner and a clear definition of done.

**Example action items from a KALM retro:**

| KALM Input | Action Item | Owner |
|------------|-------------|-------|
| Add: Pair programming on complex stories | Pair on all stories estimated 5+ points | Tech lead |
| Less: Context switching from Slack pings | Use focus blocks 10am–12pm, status in channel | Whole team |
| More: Early design reviews | Design review before sprint planning for UI stories | Designer |

Document action items and review them at the start of the next retrospective. Tools like [SprintsPlans](https://sprintsplans.com) help teams gather KALM feedback online, vote anonymously on priorities, and [track action items](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/turn-retrospective-feedback-into-action-items) across sprints.

## KALM Retrospective Examples

### Example 1: Software Development Team

**Keep:**
- Morning standups staying under 15 minutes
- Feature branch naming convention
- QA involvement during development, not just at the end

**Add:**
- Automated deployment to staging
- Sprint goal visible on the team dashboard
- Buffer time for unexpected bugs

**Less:**
- Meetings without clear agendas
- Scope changes mid-sprint
- Manual regression testing for unchanged areas

**More:**
- Code review turnaround (target: same day)
- Celebration of shipped features in team channel
- Cross-team communication before integration work

### Example 2: Remote Marketing Team

**Keep:**
- Async updates in the project tool
- Friday wins thread in Slack
- Shared content calendar

**Add:**
- Monthly cross-functional planning session
- Templates for recurring deliverables
- Video messages for complex feedback

**Less:**
- Back-to-back video calls
- Last-minute revision requests without timeline impact discussion
- Duplicate work across sub-teams

**More:**
- Early stakeholder reviews on drafts
- Peer feedback before client delivery
- Documentation of campaign learnings

## KALM vs. Other Retrospective Formats

| Format | Columns | Best When |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| KALM | Keep, Add, Less, More | Balanced improvement across four dimensions |
| [Start Stop Continue](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/start-stop-continue-retrospective-template) | Start, Stop, Continue | Simple, first-time teams |
| [4Ls](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/4ls-retrospective-template) | Liked, Learned, Lacked, Longed For | Learning-focused reflection |
| [Starfish](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/starfish-retrospective-template) | Start, Stop, Continue, More, Less | Similar to KALM with different framing |
| [DAKI](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/daki-retrospective-template) | Drop, Add, Keep, Improve | Teams ready for sharper language |

KALM and Starfish overlap significantly. Choose based on which language resonates with your team. "Keep" feels more positive than "Continue" for some groups. "Less" feels less absolute than "Stop."

## Tips for Effective KALM Retrospectives

### Balance the Columns

Teams naturally gravitate toward Less and Add — fixing problems and requesting resources. Actively prompt Keep and More. Celebrating what works builds morale and prevents teams from dismantling effective practices while chasing improvements.

### Watch for "Add" Overload

The Add column can become a wish list of tools, hires, and process changes the team cannot control. Facilitators should redirect unrealistic Adds: "What can we do within our control to address this?"

### Connect Less and More

Sometimes a Less item pairs with a More item. Less meeting time might pair with More async documentation. Surfacing these connections helps the team see trade-offs clearly.

### Use KALM for Team Health Checks

The four columns map naturally to [team health checks](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/team-health-checks). Run a KALM retrospective quarterly with a broader lens — not just the last sprint, but the last quarter.

### Adapt for Remote Teams

Remote teams benefit from digital boards with the four columns clearly labeled. Anonymous input helps when [psychological safety](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/psychological-safety-remote-teams) is still developing. Online retrospective tools make voting and action item tracking seamless for distributed teams.

## Common Facilitation Challenges

### Empty "Keep" Column

If nobody writes Keep items, ask directly: "What is one thing that went better this sprint than last sprint?" Teams in crisis mode often overlook positives.

### Repetitive Items Sprint After Sprint

If the same Less items appear every retro without action, stop generating new items and focus the entire session on why previous commitments were not followed through. [Accountability without blame](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/accountability-without-blame) is key.

### Conflicting Keep and Less

When the team disagrees on whether to keep or reduce something, facilitate a structured debate. List pros and cons. Vote. Commit to an experiment for one sprint and revisit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is KALM different from Start Stop Continue?

KALM adds "Less" and "More" as distinct from "Stop" and "Continue." Less implies reduction rather than elimination. More explicitly asks teams to invest in successes. The framing encourages balanced reflection.

### How long does a KALM retrospective take?

60–75 minutes for a two-week sprint. Scale proportionally for longer sprints. See [retrospective frequency and length](https://sprintsplans.com/blog/retrospective-frequency-and-length) for guidance.

### Can I combine KALM with other formats?

Yes. Some facilitators use KALM for the gather phase and dot voting for prioritization, then switch to a root cause format for the top issue. Mix formats when it serves the team's needs.

### Is KALM suitable for project retrospectives?

Absolutely. KALM works for sprint retrospectives, project close-outs, and quarterly reviews. Adjust the time horizon in your prompts — "this sprint" vs. "this project."

### What tools work for online KALM retrospectives?

Any tool with columns or categories works. SprintsPlans provides structured retrospective boards where teams can add cards to Keep, Add, Less, and More columns, vote anonymously, and assign action items.

## Start Your Next Retrospective with KALM

The KALM retrospective template gives teams a balanced framework for improvement. Keep what works. Add what is missing. Reduce what wastes time. Amplify what drives results.

It is simple enough to run without extensive preparation yet structured enough to produce real change. Try it in your next sprint retrospective, follow through on the action items, and see whether the four-column lens opens conversations your team was not having before.
